Buying your first home in Winnipeg is exciting — and, let's be honest, a little
overwhelming. This guide walks you through the whole journey in plain language,
with the Manitoba-specific details that actually matter.

## 1. Get your finances ready

Before you fall in love with a listing, know what you can comfortably afford.
Pull together your income documents, review your credit, and think about how
much you've saved for a down payment and closing costs.

In Canada, the minimum down payment is **5% on the first $500,000** of the price
and **10% on the portion above that**. Put down less than 20% and you'll pay
CMHC mortgage default insurance — added to your mortgage and spread across your
payments.

## 2. Get pre-approved

A mortgage pre-approval is your single most useful first step. It:

- confirms a realistic budget,
- locks in a rate for a set period (protection if rates rise), and
- makes your offers far more credible to sellers.

Talk to a mortgage broker or your bank, and bring the pre-approval to our first
meeting so we shop with confidence.

## 3. Search with a plan

This is the fun part. We'll clarify your must-haves versus nice-to-haves, pick
the Winnipeg neighbourhoods that fit your lifestyle and budget, and tour homes
together. I'll flag anything that could affect value or cost down the road.

> Tip: visit a neighbourhood at different times of day. Morning quiet and
> evening traffic can tell very different stories.

## 4. Make an offer

When you find the one, we prepare a competitive, protected offer — price,
possession date, deposit, and conditions (financing and inspection are the
common ones). I negotiate on your behalf to get you the best terms.

## 5. Satisfy your conditions

After acceptance, you'll typically have a short window to finalize financing and
complete a home inspection. If something unexpected turns up, we revisit the
deal together — this is exactly what conditions are for.

## 6. Budget for closing costs

Beyond the down payment, plan for:

- **Manitoba Land Transfer Tax** (tiered, paid at closing),
- legal fees and disbursements,
- title insurance,
- the home inspection, and
- prorated property taxes.

A common rule of thumb is roughly **1.5%–4% of the purchase price**. I'll give
you a tailored estimate so there are no surprises.

## 7. Possession day

Your lawyer handles the final paperwork and the transfer of funds, and then the
keys are yours. Welcome home.
